## Pick list — sorted by complexity

RefriTrak-anchored questions reworded to broader career framing. Warm up on the easy ones; save the hard ones for when you're in the mood to sit with them.

### Easy (7)

1. What's one thing you will not ship, even if it means missing a deadline?
2. What's a value you hold that you think would surprise people who only know your resume?
3. What's something that reliably makes you laugh out loud, not just smile?
4. Are you sharper at 6am or 10pm — and has that changed over your career?
5. Where do your to-dos actually live — app, notebook, whiteboard — and what made you land on that instead of something else?
6. Walk me through what your first 30 minutes look like on a normal workday.
7. Who's waiting for you at home, and what's the first thing you do when you walk in the door after a long day?

### Medium (10)

1. What's a business practice you've seen other founders use to grow faster that you've deliberately refused to use yourself?
2. What does "inspect what you expect" look like in practice — walk me through the last time you caught something because you went and looked instead of trusting a status update.
3. What's the physical sensation you notice first when you realize a deadline is about to be blown — before you've even decided what to do about it?
4. When was the last time you had to deliver hard feedback to someone you genuinely liked — how did you handle it?
5. Have you noticed your social energy shift over your career — more comfortable one-on-one, or more comfortable rallying a room?
6. Who's someone you can sit in a room with and say nothing for twenty minutes, and it's not weird?
7. When was the last time you scrapped a plan halfway through and improvised on the spot — what changed your mind?
8. How do you keep yourself honest about a deadline when nobody's checking in on you?
9. What's a skill or hobby you're bad at on purpose — something you do just because you enjoy it, not because you're good at it?
10. What's a side project or "side quest" you're working on right now that has zero chance of ever making money?

### Hard (28)

**Self-awareness / blind spots:**
1. If I asked someone you managed at TruClear or A-Gas what your biggest weakness as a leader was, what would they say — and would they be right?
2. Tell me about a time you gave someone else the "do it right the first time" talk and then didn't follow it yourself.
3. If your team wrote you an anonymous review right now, what's the one line you'd be nervous to read?
4. Tell me about a time "extend trust, inspect what you expect" backfired on you — where did you extend trust too far?
5. What's a blind spot you know you have but haven't fully fixed?
6. What's something you're good at that you actually think you're overrated for?
7. When was the last time you were the reason a project or deal fell apart, and how did you find out?

**Values / integrity:**
8. Have you ever been tempted to take a shortcut on quality, safety, or data handling that you talked yourself out of? What stopped you?
9. Have you ever fired or let go of someone specifically because of a values violation rather than a performance one? What was the line they crossed?
10. Describe a moment in the Marine Corps where "adapt and overcome" actually meant bending a rule you'd normally never bend. How did you decide it was okay?

**Composure / stress:**
11. What's the closest one of your companies or ops turnarounds has come to failing, and how did you feel in the days right after — not the recovery, the actual feeling?
12. What's a piece of pressure you're carrying right now that you haven't said out loud to anyone?
13. Who or what do you actually lean on when you're maxed out — not who you're supposed to say, but who you actually call or what you actually do?
14. Tell me about a moment at TruClear, in the Marines, or somewhere else where you were the only one in the room who seemed calm — what was actually going on inside you?
15. What's something you do under deadline pressure that you'd never admit to a client or investor watching?
16. Is there a kind of failure that still bothers you months later, even after you've fixed the practical fallout?

**Interpersonal / conflict:**
17. What's a disagreement with a boss or direct report where you were right, but being right wasn't the point?
18. Is there a type of conflict you actively avoid, even when you know avoiding it is the wrong call?

20. ~~Do you consider yourself more introverted or extroverted now than you were before the Marine Corps — and what changed it?~~ _(skipped — same reason)_
21. Tell me about the last real disagreement you had with a boss or direct report. Did you say what you were actually thinking in the room, or did you process it afterward and come back?

**Work discipline:**
22. What's something you started building or founding that you deliberately walked away from before finishing?
23. What's a task you keep on your list that you know you're avoiding — and how long has it been sitting there?
24. Tell me about a stretch where you were heads-down building and let ops or people-management slide. How did you know it was time to come back up for air?
25. ~~What's a habit from the Marine Corps that still shapes how you structure a workday, and one that you deliberately dropped?~~ _(skipped — Marines-anchored)_

**Life outside:**

27. If your kids or spouse described what "Dad on a good weekend" looks like, what would they say?

## Voice gaps

Where the current voice guide is **untested**, not wrong:

1. **Casual peer/team acknowledgment warmth** — no sample of Daniel responding in the moment to a teammate. Unknown whether em-dash-heavy contrast formality relaxes at Slack length. _Prompt 0._
2. **Customer-facing warmth vs. fix-and-move-on directness** — no sample writing TO an unhappy customer. Does he apologize, over-explain, or just fix? _Prompt 3._
3. **External persuasive / marketing / recruiting register** — no persuasive copy for outside readers. "Benefit-forward, zero-hedge" untested. _Prompts 1, 4._
4. **Real-time concision before the outcome is known** — every current pattern is retrospective (STAR, "bottom line"). Does the distillation habit survive when the outcome is still open? _Prompts 5, 6._
5. **Public broadcast pride calibration** — "confident, not bragging" and "no exclamation points" derived only from 1:1 interview answers. Untested in tweet/LinkedIn. _Prompts 2, 8._
6. **Directive/command authority voice in writing** — the corpus narrates past leadership. A reorg announcement or decision memo requires writing AS the person exercising authority — present-tense, declarative. Untested. _Prompts 7, 9._
